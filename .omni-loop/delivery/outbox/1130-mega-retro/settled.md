## The question, in plain words

The app ships a prebuilt copy of its code that a test keeps up to date, and the plan did not list that copy as ground this slice may touch. Is it fine that the slice rebuilt it?

## The decision, in plain words

The slice rebuilt the app's prebuilt copy with the repository's own build command and committed it, so the test that guards it stays green.

## The intro, for fun

The plan drew a fence around the slice, and the app's prebuilt copy sat just outside it.

## The punchline, for fun

We stepped over to dust it off, and touched nothing else on the way back.

## The options, in plain words

A. A. Rebuild the copy in the slice that changes the app, as done.
B. B. Leave it stale and let a later step rebuild it, with the guarding test red until then.
C. C. Name the copy in the plan's ground for every slice that touches the app.

## What I had to decide

Whether a slice that changes the app may always rebuild its prebuilt copy, or whether plans should name that copy as part of the ground.

## What I did meanwhile

The rebuilt copy is committed on the slice branch; it is generated, never edited by hand.

## What it costs to change later

Nothing to undo: the copy is regenerated by one command on any later change.

## The question, in plain words

When a repository received the work in several stacked deliveries, the retro reads the work of all of them but takes the last delivery's pull request as that repository's feature PR. Should each delivery get its own timeline and churn instead?

## The decision, in plain words

Each repository is read once: every slice of every delivery counts, and the last delivery's pull request stands for the repository's feature PR in its timeline and final diff.

## The intro, for fun

A repository that took the work in three trips still gets one chapter in the retro.

## The punchline, for fun

The last trip signs the chapter, and the earlier ones are in the footnotes.

## The options, in plain words

A. A. One reading per repository, the last delivery standing for its feature PR, as built.
B. B. One reading per delivery of each repository, each with its own timeline and churn.
C. C. One reading per repository, its timeline spanning from the first delivery's opening to the last one's merge.

## What I had to decide

Whether a repository built in several deliveries is described once, or once per delivery.

## What I did meanwhile

Every delivery's pull request is listed under that repository; its timeline and final diff read only the last one.

## What it costs to change later

Small: one more loop over the deliveries in the per-repository read, and one more level in the grouped sections.

## What I could not know

(author) The PRD, the registers and the glossary do not settle this:

- No plan of several deliveries across repositories has run yet, so how often this matters is not known (author).

A finding about another repository needs a name that cannot clash with the same kind of finding in the plan repository. Is prefixing it with that repository's short name, and its title with the repository's full name, the right way?

## The decision, in plain words

A finding about another repository has the repository's short name in front of its name and its full name in front of its title, and all findings are ranked together, most severe first.

## The intro, for fun

Two repositories can both have a slow slice called s3.

## The punchline, for fun

Now each slow slice wears a name tag, so nobody mixes up whose it is.

## The options, in plain words

A. A. Prefix the name with the short repository name and the title with the full one, ranked across repositories, as built.
B. B. Keep the names as they are and number the findings within each repository.
C. C. Prefix the name only, and leave the title for the issue to name the repository.

## What I had to decide

How a finding about another repository is named and titled, and whether findings are ranked across repositories or within each one.

## What I did meanwhile
