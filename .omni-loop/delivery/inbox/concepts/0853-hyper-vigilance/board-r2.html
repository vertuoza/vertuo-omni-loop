Win the skeptic: the AI catches itself

Your answer to round 1: “all of those a velocity killer … as soon as ai needs a human for opinion of an old developers - we kills the velocity … not so much a the ai did wrong except maybe the 2 agents”. The rule since: nothing waits on a human’s opinion during the build; the skeptic is won by evidence that the AI finds what it did wrong and fixes it itself; today’s gates stay as they are. G is carried in from round 1; three new artists drew six concepts (I–N) as storyboard cards, since you chose to go wide again. Feasibility is scored from this round.

How the panel reads the set, as the moderator heard it. Six of seven check the loop against something no model chose: the team’s past bugs (I), the product’s own rules (L), its other outputs (M), injected failures (N), production (J). The panel converged on one shape it calls “Never twice”: before merge, L everywhere there is computation and I wherever files have a fix history, picked by territory, scored on I’s replay of this repository’s history, run in parallel so a slice waits for the slowest check only; M nightly; after merge, K turns every escape into a recall and a new guard. A line ledger that survives squash merges is built first. J and N become specialist tiers; G retires (its cross-test lives on as I’s planted bugs).

Dissents kept on the shape. Value: the risk label is the loop’s own judgment, so it must not pick the checks; each check earns its CI minutes by catches per minute on I’s replay, falls to nightly below the bar, and every sub-PR prints its wall time next to its catches. Skeptic: “0 escapes” and “27 of 27 caught” are true by construction; the headline must be the miss rate per area, as a trend. PM: L ships first, not the ledger — the engineer must merge this week. Visionary: build L first, crown K. Owner: one small recall PR per area, never one big one. You decide.
