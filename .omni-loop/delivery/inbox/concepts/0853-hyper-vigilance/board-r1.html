Think big · Round 1 · go wide · 8 concepts

Win the skeptic

The brief. A senior engineer who distrusts agent-written code chooses Omni Loop for real work. Two objections to beat: quality (“slop, I rewrite it anyway”) and control (“agents touch my code without me”). The game is left alone. Four concept artists (10×, steal from another industry, delight, contrarian) drew two concepts each; a panel of Visionary, Craft, Skeptic, Value and two users (the owner, the PM) debated them over two turns. Feasibility is not scored in round 1.

How the panel reads the set, as the moderator heard it. F is the control frame most would build on; E the answer to slop; B the record that makes both honest; C the moment people would retell; D the owner’s memory. A, G and H were mostly argued as parts of those rather than concepts on their own. The panel does not agree on what to crown: see each dissent. You decide: keep, kill, merge or push each one.

Stances

  • Visionary The learning spine — and the loop posts its own post-mortem on the old sub-PR.
  • Craft The record, but only on a line ledger; the claim arrives on the owner’s rewrite PR, not a place to visit.
  • Skeptic The trust spine. Smallest proof: a line ledger that survives squash merges, computed over PRDs already shipped.
  • Value The proof a skeptic shows a peer: “the loop’s lines here, 88% unchanged at 60 days”.
  • Owner The record under everything — with one key per claim: bug, taste, or the spec moved.
  • PM Computed on history, it pays on the first PR; a “spec moved” claim comes to me.

Consensus. Unanimous: the record under whatever wins. It needs a line ledger (git blame lies after a squash merge), a backfill over shipped PRDs so day one shows real history, and a bug / taste / spec-moved key so refactors never count against the loop.

Dissent. None against it. The early timing doubt (payoff 19 days after merge) was answered by the Skeptic’s backfill.

Best remix. Run it on the past first: “lines still as the loop wrote them, 60 days on” for every shipped PRD, on the owner’s first screen (Skeptic).

Stances

Stances

  • Visionary Quote only “rebuilt to hold your rule”: where a first draft broke it.
  • Craft Quote on failure, never on compliance, or it becomes the wall of comments.
  • Skeptic Yes if “held” means a check ran (ast-grep, a lint rule, a test); no if it’s the loop’s say-so in a nicer font.
  • Value Pays on day one from review history — once a real check stands behind every quote.
  • Owner Scoped to my turf; “held” must mean a check ran; stale rules come back to me.
  • PM Brings the owner’s words into the phase-0 PR; exceptions only.

Consensus. Harvest owners’ review words into rules kept verbatim, made into checks that actually run; quote the owner only where a draft broke the rule and was rebuilt. Never used to police colleagues’ human PRs (all six agree, after the owner objected).

Dissent. Craft and the Skeptic: as drawn it is reassurance, not evidence — a margin of compliance quotes sinks under Tricorder’s noise bar.

Best remix. Every rule that can be mechanised becomes a check whose failure message is the owner’s quote; the rest are labelled “judged, not checked” (Skeptic).

  • Skeptic The mutant receipt is real evidence; precedent and spec receipts must prove a low post-merge miss rate first.

Consensus. The panel’s answer to slop: every changed (and deleted) line earns a receipt — a mutant its tests kill, a precedent from stable code, the spec line or law it serves — and the owner rates each receipt useful / not useful.

Best remix. Audit receipts after merge: per receipt kind, how often a “paid” line was later rewritten — retire any kind that misses the bar (Skeptic).

Best remix. The promotion is a one-line PR against turf.yml, reviewed like code (Visionary); the turf cost of a PRD appears in its phase-0 PR (PM).

  • Skeptic Put the brief on every sub-PR; drop the callout ritual and the guessed “~40 min”.
